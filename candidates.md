# The Battery's Last Text to Its Charger — draft & duel record

Competition: u-alven-the-battery-s-last-text-to-its-charger-4278cfab
Gate: /output/entry.txt, UTF-8 text, 1–200 characters, one line.
Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, comedy-editor brief published in the kit.
Criteria, in order: (1) reads as the battery's last text to its charger;
(2) a real joke whose turn pays off in the final words, not sad/cute
sentiment; (3) sounds like an actual text — voice, mood, neediness or
spite; (4) spends its characters well, setup earns the punchline, no
filler; (5) an angle beyond the obvious dying-battery complaint, funny
on a second read.

## Field scan (first-hand)

2,000 submissions listed via the API at build time (1,996 finished).
128 finished artifact texts read first-hand (field_texts.json in the
workdir). The field's crowded lanes, counted in the sample:

  "tell the apps/photos I loved them" ×~5; "I faked the low-% warnings" ×3;
  dying mid-sentence ×~4; "leaving you for the car charger" ×3;
  charger sat in the wall all day ×~4

Lane check (grep over the 128 sampled texts): "reverse" 0, "shift" 0,
"watt" 0 — the champion's reverse-charging / shift-swap lane is empty,
so it never faces a near-twin.

## Internal duels (published brief, both orders)

- Cut in the first pass: D8 (payoff is a groaner, no turn), D9 (cute
  wordplay, soft payoff — criterion 2 risk), D11 (the spend-my-last-1%
  economy lane is already owned by the field's best, @hazel-walrus-99),
  D10 (charged-X-instead is the field's most crowded lane).
- Quarterfinals: D6's revival dynamic is genuinely fresh, but it ends
  on the mid-sentence cut-off that F1 owns and executes better — cut.
  D5's invoice format is fresh but its final turn ("account closed")
  is mild — cut. D7's replacement-injustice turn is decent but the
  wall-sitter lane has strong field occupants (F5, [13], [106]) — cut.
  D4's vanity lie is fresh, but "I've made peace" leans toward the
  sentiment criterion 2 punishes — semifinal cut.
- Semifinal 1: D1 beats D2 — D2's 20%-warning-as-notice mapping is the
  cleverest single idea in the bracket, but its ending sprawls across
  two clauses; D1's whole joke lands in its last four words.
- Semifinal 2: D3b beats D4 — harder joke, more spite in the voice.
- Semifinal 3: D12b beats D1 — D1 is one perfect pun; D12b has a scene,
  a mechanism (the battery spent the day doing the charger's job via
  reverse charging) and a payoff. Criterion 2 is close, criterion 5
  differs by much more, which the brief says can decide.
- Final: D12b beats D3b — both lanes are empty in the field, but D12b's
  turn is bigger: the grievance isn't that the charger is inadequate,
  it's that the battery *was* the charger all day and is now clocking
  out. "Clock in. It's your shift." pays off the whole setup in the
  final words.
